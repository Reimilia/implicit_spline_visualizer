What each algorithm computes

The occupancy is u, the Ambrosio–Tortorelli edge field is a, and q = (u, a) is the configuration with conjugate momentum p. The received data and confidence are g and w. The potential V uses the AT term V_AT, the neural term V_N, or both, depending on the method. In the pH equations, H is the Hamiltonian, M is mass, D is momentum damping, α is configuration dissipation, and G is the feedback metric. The operator Dₕ denotes grid differences, and ⟨·⟩ denotes the grid average.

Saved numerical results, with the conventions used in the benchmark.

One state, every view

The contour, chart cursors, phase marker, and readouts use the same recorded state. Replay holds saved states between samples. Vertical energy changes at an observation boundary are preserved.

A projection of phase space

The phase portrait uses a fixed direction from the shared initial field to the final observed field, normalized in the grid L² pairing. It is a display projection of the field and its momentum, rather than the complete phase space.

Two kinds of solver clock

Flows use numerical relaxation units. Split Bregman uses iterations. The horizontal alignment shows observation-stage progress; it does not equate iterations with physical seconds or identify a true deformation history.

What the references mean

IoU and the dashed scoring contour use the clean synthetic target for evaluation only. Topology counts include every component and hole, with 8-connected foreground and 4-connected background. Clean labels do not enter the solvers.
